The [Shivas Irons Society](https://shivas.org/) is the spirit-over-score argument organized into a 501(c)(3). Founded in 1992 at the U.S. Open at Pebble Beach by readers of Michael Murphy's _Golf in the Kingdom_, it exists to champion the inner game—golf as a path of mastery and personal growth rather than a card to be signed. That belief is upstream of our own: the C&RBGC's insistence that a bogey is no disgrace is a small and practical version of the Society's larger claim that the score is the least of what golf is for.

The present deserves honesty, and it is the reason this note hedges where the others do not. The Society today reads less like a grassroots community and more like a tour operator with a philosophy—its year is a handful of curated, fairly expensive pilgrimages, the Scotland trip and the Games of the Links at [Bandon Dunes](/notes/dream-golf-and-cabot-portfolio/) chief among them, run out of a small office. The temptation is to call it dormant, and from the outside it can look that way: the social channels have gone quiet and the event pages carry stale dates. But the calendar says otherwise—through 2026 it has run a members' event and a "True Gravity" camp at Quail, an Ojai and Santa Barbara outing, and a Scotland trip that sold out months in advance. Mark is a member, and the membership is better described as hyper-local to those few gatherings than dead. We keep it on the list for the argument it carries, which is one we believe.
