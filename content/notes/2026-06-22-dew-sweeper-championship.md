A dew sweeper is the golfer in the first group out, walking before the maintenance crew, leaving tracks across a course that still belongs to no one. The Dew Sweeper Championship makes a tournament of that hour. It is contested at first light near the summer solstice, when dawn comes earliest and the longest day rewards anyone willing to meet it—tee times that begin in the half-dark, shoes soaked through by the second hole, the course wholly to ourselves.

The genius of the format is its qualifying standard, which is simply showing up. There is no entry but the alarm clock, and no real advantage to the low handicapper, because what it measures is not skill but the willingness to be there at all. The truly dedicated win it by being the kind of golfer who would play at that hour anyway.

What the early start protects is the older pleasure underneath the game: a walk in the cool, in good company, before the day has asked anything of you. See [Golf's Original Constraints](/notes/golfs-original-constraints/).
