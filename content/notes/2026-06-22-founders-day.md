Once a year the Club pauses to remember that it was founded on purpose. Mark Ayers and Dean Chase did not set out to build another scorecard-and-handicap club; they set out to organize a fellowship around walking, camaraderie, and the target of bogey—match play, hole by hole, against [Colonel Bogey](/notes/colonel-bogey-and-the-bogey-competition/)—and then, with characteristic seriousness about an unserious thing, gave it a constitution and adopted _Robert's Rules_ to run it by.

Founders' Day is the round played in that spirit. There is no special format and no prize; the point is the founding motto, said aloud on the first tee and meant—"We can make bogey here, right?"—and a toast afterward to the two who decided the game was worth organizing on these terms rather than the usual ones.

It is, admittedly, a little absurd for a club this size to hold a day in honor of its own founding. The founders would be the first to say so, which is precisely why the day is kept.
