A scramble is the team format in which everyone tees off, the side picks the best ball, and all play their next from there. It is the staple of the charity outing and the corporate day, and the Club's opinion of it as _golf_ is a settled and gentle disdain.

The objection is not snobbery; it is arithmetic. The Club's only register is the margin against [Colonel Bogey](/notes/colonel-bogey-and-the-bogey-competition/)—your own ball, hole by hole, an honest result you have to own. A scramble abolishes exactly that. You never make your bogey; you make the team's borrowed birdie, and the card records a number that describes nobody's round. "A bogey is an honest result" is the founding conviction, and a scramble's twelve-under is the honest result of nothing. It is fantasy scoring in a format descended from the beer-cart and the cart path rather than from the commons and the links. For the Club's purposes, a scramble is not a round that happened.

And yet we recognize what it is genuinely good at, which is letting everyone in. The scramble is the most socially available form the game has: it carries the beginner, the once-a-year player, and the friend who came for the company and not the golf, and it carries them around the course laughing, with nobody exposed and nobody holding up the group. That is not a small virtue in a game that too often guards its door. The Club's whole politics is that golf should be open and walkable and unpretentious, and the scramble, whatever its sins against scoring, is open. So we do not scold it. We play one cheerfully when the occasion calls for a crowd, and we count it for exactly what it is: an afternoon of fellowship, not a test of golf.

What it will never be is a counting round for [the Colonel's Cup](/notes/the-colonels-cup/). When the Club wants a team format that is still honest golf, it reaches for foursomes—alternate shot, one ball per side, every stroke owned—already blessed in the [Match Play Guide](/notes/match-play-guide/).
